INT. HARBOR FERRY TERMINAL — NIGHT

Rain hammers the windows. DANNY, sixties, counts the cash drawer. MAYA bursts in, soaked.

MAYA: Please tell me the 9:40 hasn't left.

DANNY: (without looking up) Left at 9:40. On the dot. Like it does every night.

MAYA: No. No, no, no. My sister is on that boat.

DANNY: Then your sister's having a lovely, uh, very wet crossing.

MAYA: You don't understand. She doesn't know I'm here. I flew in from Denver this morning just to say I'm sorry.

DANNY: (finally looks up) Sorry for what?

MAYA: For not picking up the phone. For three years.

Danny studies her. Then he reaches for the radio.

DANNY: Pier 6, this is the ticket office. How far out is the Marianne?

RADIO: Ticket office, she never cleared the breakwater. Engine trouble. We're bringing her back in.

MAYA: Wait. She's coming back?

DANNY: Pier 6. Ten minutes. Gate C, past the vending machines.

MAYA: I don't even know what I'm going to say to her.

DANNY: Kid, you came two thousand miles. Start with hello.

MAYA: Hello. Okay. I can do hello.

She runs for Gate C.
